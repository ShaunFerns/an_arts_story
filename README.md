# First Semester: An Arts Story

### Explore a campus. Meet the people. Find your way through Arts.

**First Semester: An Arts Story** is an interactive, three-dimensional campus experience exploring how students encounter university life, academic choices and the people who support them during their first semester.

Set in a stylised version of **TU Dublin's Grangegorman campus**, the experience invites players to arrive by Luas, find their way to East Quad, take part in an induction challenge and become involved in the questions and decisions that arise as new BA Arts students settle into university.

**[Explore *First Semester: An Arts Story*](https://shaunferns.github.io/an_arts_story/)**

## The idea

The transition to university involves much more than finding a classroom or reading programme information. Students are learning how to navigate an unfamiliar environment, recognise opportunities, understand academic choices, and decide when and where to seek advice.

*An Arts Story* explores whether an interactive narrative can help students encounter these situations before—or alongside—their own first-semester experiences. Information about the university and its programme is introduced through places, conversations and decisions, with space for curiosity, uncertainty and reflection.

The experience takes inspiration from the exploratory nature of the **BA Arts (TU980)** programme. At the beginning of their studies, students are encouraged to consider a range of subject interests while becoming familiar with the structures and requirements that shape their future options. Academic advising helps students make sense of those possibilities as their understanding develops.

## What players experience

- **Arriving and belonging.** Begin at the Broadstone–University Luas stop, approach East Quad and meet people who help make the campus more familiar.
- **Learning the campus.** Explore locations including the Student Hub, Students' Union, library and other Grangegorman landmarks through an induction scavenger hunt.
- **Noticing and reflecting.** Choose where to go, make observations about campus life and record field notes rather than simply follow a prescribed tour.
- **Making sense of academic choices.** Encounter fellow students with questions about subject interests, preferences, timetables and future pathways.
- **Finding support.** Discover how conversations with academic staff and university services can help students investigate a concern and make an informed next step.

These elements are being developed as parts of a connected story. The intention is for people and situations to become more familiar as the first semester unfolds, and for different choices to shape subsequent encounters.

## Educational approach

The project is guided by an interest in **learning through participation, exploration and reflection**.

The virtual campus provides a setting for situated encounters. The narrative introduces questions that a new student might plausibly face. Interactive decisions encourage the player to gather information, weigh alternatives and recognise that academic choices are made within a programme structure and a community of support.

The educational ambition is to complement conventional induction and advising materials by giving students opportunities to **practise navigating uncertainty**. The game is also a space to investigate which forms of narrative, feedback and interaction are genuinely useful, rather than assuming that a 3D environment is educationally effective simply because it is engaging.

## An evolving educational exploration

*First Semester: An Arts Story* is an **early-stage, independently developed prototype**. Its Grangegorman setting is stylised, and the characters and situations are constructed for the experience. The project is being developed through iterative design and review; its educational effectiveness has not yet been established through student evaluation.

The programme-related situations are intended to prompt exploration and discussion. **The game is not an official source of programme rules, module availability, timetables or individual academic advice.** Students should rely on current TU Dublin guidance and their academic adviser for those matters.

The longer-term aim is to explore the value of interactive storytelling as a complement to academic advising and first-year transition support, informed by feedback from students and colleagues.
