# First Semester: An Arts Story — induction sequence

## Educational intent

The opening of *First Semester: An Arts Story* uses the first visit to Grangegorman to help new BA Arts students become familiar with the campus, university people and services, other students, academic advising and the practical sequence leading into Semester 1.

The opening should be more than navigation or an entertaining video. Its value lies in giving players opportunities to explore and begin understanding the university, the programme and their own developing interests.

## Story rhythm

| Moment | What happens | Intended learning |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival by Luas | Short animated Third-person 3D sequence at Broadstone–University. Doors open; first years step onto the platform; the camera reveals a broad campus. Scene is skippable. | First orientation, scale and sense of arrival |
| Walk to East Quad | Player moves independently through campus with natural camera controls. | Navigating a real-world university environment |
| BA Arts welcome | Bríd introduces new classmates, programme staff, student services and the purpose of the induction day. | Belonging, help-seeking and the difference between services and staff roles |
| Academic adviser conversation | Adviser invites the player to express **indicative academic preferences for Semester 1** and questions/constraints. | Articulating interests while understanding that preferences are not confirmed allocations or a formal Major declaration |
| Sustainability scavenger hunt | Player chooses a route, observes places and records notes about student supports, university life, sustainability, study and wellbeing. | Learning campus geography and the human context of university services |
| Induction debrief | Return to East Quad to compare observations; recognise that other students may notice different things. | Reflection, connecting places to purposes and helping peers |
| **Friday before classes begin** | Short scene moves the story forward. **Programme Operations publishes initial timetables**, informed by preferences, programme constraints and module availability. | Understanding when and how indicative preferences become an actual timetable |
| Cian’s first timetable question | A subject he ranked highly is missing; the player investigates the difference between a preference, timetable and allocation. | Evidence before assumption; distinguishing formal administrative issues from academic pathway questions |
| Further semester encounters | Saoirse discovers an unanticipated subject interest and returns to academic advising. | Changing interests and future possibilities without promising immediate module swaps |

## Representation and safeguards

- In the story, the **academic adviser captures and discusses interests and pathway considerations**; **Programme Operations assembles and publishes the initial timetable**. Avoid suggesting an adviser can directly assign or guarantee modules.
- First-year academic interests are provisional. Do not call this a formal Major confirmation.
- Only show the player's initial timetable once the story has passed the Friday publication point.
- Any timetable displayed by the game is **illustrative**; it is not a real student timetable or a source of live module availability.
- Render student services as useful relationships and places. The player should meet or observe people, not simply receive a list of office names.
- Keep animated transitions brief and skippable. Preserve camera control, accessibility and manual navigation outside scenes.
- Characters, social behaviour and environmental details should be credible to contemporary first-year students, with diversity expressed through natural clothing and varied activity rather than exaggerated stereotypes.
- Seek student and adviser feedback before making claims about effectiveness.
