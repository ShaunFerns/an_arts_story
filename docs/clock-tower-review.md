# Clock Tower Building

The game depicts the surviving complex on Grangegorman Lower. Appendix 2a §A2a.3 describes the original plan, the fabric that survived into the survey and the condition at the time it was written. Figure A2a:4 is a measured ground-floor plan of the complex as it stood at survey. The 2025 photographs and the GDA conservation record help distinguish that survey from the current street elevation and use.

## Comparison and changes

| Feature | Previous game model | Evidence and revised model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Footprint and orientation | The mapped perimeter located the building east of Grangegorman Lower. The model assigned a single wall height and a single hip to that whole outline. | Six identifiable outer corners on Figure A2a:4 give a scale of about 0.0945 game metres per pixel on a 2,000 px render and an RMS plan registration residual of about 0.95 m. The north arrow and the geotagged 2025 street photographs support the orientation of the long west front. The game retains the mapped perimeter for collision and clips the separate roofs to it. |
| West elevation | Generic bay spacing followed each map edge. A separate pediment marked the centre without controlling the elevation below it. | Three storeys across 17 bays in a 6–5–6 arrangement. The centre projects and has a pediment over all five bays. Subtle end projections, sash divisions, granite sills and string course, the porch with a segmental arch and balcony rail, and recessed carriageway locations are now drawn as part of the street elevation. The north doorway is represented with its timber gates closed. |
| Roof | One broad profile covered wings, spine and rear appendages. Stack positions and heights lacked a building-specific layout. | Hipped slate roofs to the west wings, a pedimented central roof, an east-running pitched roof over the chapel link, a higher north–south gable over the north return and low service roofs at the east end. The GDA aerial gives the roof directions and relative levels. Eaves and ridge heights are approximate. |
| Clock tower | A small cupola was fitted to the shared roof, with a simplified clock drum. | The chamfered square granite tower stands on the central bay axis. It has four clock faces, a copper ogee dome and a weather vane. Its vertical proportions were estimated from the recent front photographs. |
| East structures and courtyards | The perimeter broadly followed the map, including the open spaces, but the uniform wall height and roof read as a single block. | Distinct canted spine and former chapel, connecting corridor, partial north return and longer east range. The east walls use separate sash and brick surrounds, with pointed upper lights on the chapel section. The roof planes run to the mapped wall lines, and masonry closes the stepped junctions between the ranges without covering the two rear courts. The grounds west of the street and the east courtyard are separate areas. The model draws the foreground rail and mapped bollards as open metalwork and individual posts. |

## Historical and surviving fabric

- Francis Johnston’s penitentiary originally had a radial plan and a five-sided enclosure. The full enclosure and radial cell wings are historical and are excluded from the campus model.
- The original west range, the canted spine with its former chapel and part of the north wing survive. The mapped east range also includes later fabric. Its mass is shown as part of the present-day perimeter. Figure A2a:4 includes ancillary footprints, some shown hatched. The game draws only volumes supported by the mapped perimeter and omits independent ancillary structures seen only in the older plan.
- The north carriageway historically led from Grangegorman Lower towards the rear courts. The conservation record documents the gates and sash repairs. The public campus model shows the gate and keeps the restricted interior closed. It leaves the rear open ground clear of building collision. Upper windows, roof junctions and the precise construction dates of individual rear sections remain provisional where recent rear photographs are unavailable.
